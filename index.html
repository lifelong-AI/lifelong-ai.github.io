About me

I am a researcher in multimodal understanding and reasoning, currently working at the Future Laboratory, Tsinghua University. I study how multimodal large language models (MLLMs) make sense of visual and auditory information, and how they use it to understand real-world situations.

My research examines how models interpret information in context and use relevant evidence to reason and make decisions. I am particularly interested in the internal representations that support these abilities, and in how models apply what they learn to new tasks and unfamiliar situations.

My earlier research focused on deep learning, time-series modeling, and human-centered AI.

Selected Projects

Multimodal understanding

PromptShield Home

Understanding instructions in everyday environments

Smart-home MLLMs need to distinguish genuine user instructions from surrounding speech and on-screen content. PromptShield Home studies this problem through realistic home scenarios, comparing how different systems interpret visual and auditory inputs and when they choose to act. The study highlights the difficulty of responding to valid requests while ignoring irrelevant background content.

Robotic perception

ShakeSort

ICRA · Submitted

Hidden-content perception through robotic shaking

ShakeSort explores how robotic shaking can help reveal hidden contents. The work examines task-dependent sensing pathways, connecting physical interaction with the information a robot needs to perceive what is inside.

Physiological signal modeling

PBG-MTFNet

EEG-based fatigue detection

PBG-MTFNet brings together spatial relationships between electrodes, frequency-band information, and temporal dynamics to detect fatigue from EEG signals. Evaluation on SEED-VIG and SADT examines both detection performance and generalization to unseen subjects.

Model design
Per-band graph convolution captures relationships between electrodes within each frequency band. Multi-scale temporal-frequency modeling combines information across bands and time.
Evaluation
Experiments on SEED-VIG and SADT use mixed-subject and leave-one-subject-out settings to examine detection performance and generalization to unseen subjects.
