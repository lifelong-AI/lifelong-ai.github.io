About

I am a research assistant at the Future Laboratory, Tsinghua University, working on multimodal understanding and reasoning. I study how models make sense of visual and auditory information, with a focus on what they represent and how those representations shape their decisions.

My current work examines whether multimodal models base their judgments on relevant evidence, and how misleading inputs affect the reliability of AI agents. More broadly, I aim to build AI that genuinely understands the world and can draw on that understanding to reason and act in unfamiliar situations.

I received my master's and bachelor's degrees in Transportation Engineering from Chang'an University, where I worked on deep learning for time-series modeling and human-state prediction.

Personal Project

PBG-MTFNet

EEG-based fatigue detection

I designed and implemented a deep learning model to detect fatigue from EEG signals, bringing together spatial relationships between electrodes, frequency-band information, and temporal dynamics.

Model design
Per-band graph convolution captures relationships between electrodes within each frequency band. Multi-scale temporal-frequency modeling combines information across bands and time.
Evaluation
I evaluated the model on SEED-VIG and SADT under mixed-subject and leave-one-subject-out settings, examining both detection performance and generalization to unseen subjects.
